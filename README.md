# CS-250: Software Development Lifecycle

## Course Overview

In CS-250: Software Development Lifecycle at Southern New Hampshire University, I explored software development methodologies, with a particular focus on Agile principles and the Scrum framework. Throughout the course, I worked on the SNHU Travel project, where I gained experience with the responsibilities of the Scrum Master, Product Owner, and Development Team. ## Portfolio Artifact: Sprint Review and Retrospective

The Sprint Review and Retrospective document demonstrates my understanding of Agile software development and the Scrum framework. It reflects on the SNHU Travel project, including the development of a Top Five Destinations feature, the importance of stakeholder feedback, and the lessons learned throughout the development process.

This artifact highlights my ability to evaluate software development practices, communicate effectively with team members, and identify opportunities for continuous improvement.

## 1. How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I believe interpreting user needs begins with understanding what the user wants to accomplish and why a particular feature is important to them. Rather than immediately writing code, I would first gather requirements, communicate with stakeholders, and identify the functionality needed to meet their expectations. Creating user stories helps organize these requirements into smaller, manageable tasks that focus on the user's perspective.

During the SNHU Travel project, I worked with a user story involving the Top Five Destinations feature. The goal was to provide users with a list of travel destinations that included descriptions, images, and clickable links to travel packages. By following the user story and its acceptance criteria, I was able to develop a Java Swing application that displayed the required information and allowed users to access the appropriate travel package pages. This experience taught me that user stories provide developers with clear objectives while helping ensure that the finished product meets user expectations.

## 2. How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

When developing programs, I prefer to begin by understanding the requirements, breaking the project into smaller tasks, and establishing a clear plan before writing code. I then focus on implementing functionality, testing the results, identifying errors, and making improvements. My experience in the Marine Corps and aviation maintenance taught me the importance of organization, attention to detail, and following established procedures. I believe these skills translate well into software development, particularly when maintaining code quality and meeting project requirements.

Through CS-250, I learned that Agile development provides the flexibility to respond to changing requirements without losing sight of the overall project goals. In my future development work, I hope to incorporate Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, Sprint Reviews, and Sprint Retrospectives. I also want to use Product Backlogs and user stories to organize and prioritize development tasks. These practices encourage communication, accountability, and continuous improvement while allowing teams to deliver functional software incrementally.

## 3. What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

I believe being a good team member in software development means communicating clearly, taking responsibility for assigned work, respecting different perspectives, and supporting the team's overall objectives. Successful software development requires more than technical knowledge. It also depends on collaboration, adaptability, and the willingness to help others overcome challenges.

My military and aviation maintenance experience taught me that teamwork is essential when completing complex tasks where accuracy and reliability are critical. I carried this perspective into the SNHU Travel project, where I learned about the responsibilities of the Scrum Master, Product Owner, and Developer. Each role contributes to the success of the project, and effective communication between these roles helps prevent misunderstandings and delays. I believe a strong software development team should encourage transparency, provide constructive feedback, and continuously look for ways to improve its processes.
